51. Inclusive Design Foundations

Design for a wide range of people, contexts and abilities from the beginning—not as a final compliance pass.

LEARN

Inclusion changes the design process

Consider differences in vision, hearing, mobility, cognition, language, technology, environment and temporary circumstances. The goal is to remove avoidable barriers while keeping the product useful and understandable.

Designer question: Who might be excluded by this assumption, and what evidence can we use to reduce that risk?

SEE
  • Design for varied abilities and contexts.
  • Make important information perceivable in more than one way.
  • Support different input methods.
  • Allow recovery when users make mistakes.
  • Test with realistic variation, not only the ideal case.
