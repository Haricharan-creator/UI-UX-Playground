Lesson 092: Usability Testing Metrics

Learn how to use task-based usability evidence to understand where an experience succeeds, where people struggle and what needs investigation.

Learn

Metrics support observation; they do not replace it

Usability testing can capture task success, time, errors, assistance and participant confidence. The useful interpretation comes from combining these signals with what people actually did, said and misunderstood.

Designer question: What happened during the task, what evidence shows the pattern, and what additional observation is needed before changing the design?
See

Testing measurement essentials

Task successDefine in advance what counts as completing the task and record partial outcomes consistently.
Time on taskUse elapsed time when speed is relevant, while considering hesitation and task complexity.
ErrorsRecord meaningful mistakes, recovery attempts and points where users need help.
AssistanceTrack when moderator intervention or hints were required, without treating every hint equally.
ConfidenceAsk participants how confident or difficult the task felt to add subjective evidence.
Observation notesCapture behaviour and reasoning so a number can be interpreted in context.

What makes usability-test metrics more useful?

Apply

Run a usability measurement exercise in Figma or Framer

Task

Choose one prototype flow. Define the task, success criteria, time measure, meaningful errors, assistance rules and one confidence question before testing.

Deliver

Create a simple observation sheet and test with representative participants where possible. Separate observed evidence from interpretation and proposed design changes.

Do not overgeneralise from a small sample. Use the findings to identify patterns and questions for further validation.
