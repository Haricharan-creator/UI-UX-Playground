Accessible Navigation & Landmarks

Learn how page structure, headings, navigation regions and consistent patterns help people understand where they are and move efficiently through an experience.

Learn

Structure is part of the interface

Visual hierarchy alone does not guarantee an understandable structure. People may navigate by headings, landmarks, menus or other structural cues. Clear information architecture and consistent navigation reduce the work needed to find the next step.

Designer question: Can someone quickly understand the page structure, identify where they are and reach the important destinations without scanning everything?
See

Navigation essentials

HeadingsUse meaningful hierarchy that reflects the content structure and task.
LandmarksSeparate major regions such as navigation, main content and complementary content.
Consistent navigationKeep recurring navigation patterns and destinations predictable across screens.
Current locationMake the user's current section or page understandable.
Skip pathsProvide efficient ways to bypass repeated content when appropriate.
Multiple routesSupport useful search, navigation and contextual links without creating unnecessary duplication.

Task

Choose a multi-screen flow. Map headings, navigation regions, current-location cues, repeated navigation and the shortest route to key tasks.

Deliver

Create a clearer information hierarchy and document navigation rules that should remain consistent across screens.
