Accessible Content & Media

Learn how writing, images, video, audio, motion and visual hierarchy can either reduce or create barriers — and how to design alternatives that preserve meaning.

Learn

Meaning should not depend on one sense

Accessible communication considers different ways people perceive and process information. Important meaning should not rely only on colour, sound, animation or imagery.

Designer question: If a person cannot see the image, hear the audio, or tolerate motion, can they still access the important meaning and complete the task?

Content and media essentials

Alt textProvide useful alternatives for meaningful images; treat decorative images appropriately.
CaptionsSupport understanding of spoken content in video.
TranscriptsProvide text alternatives for relevant audio/video content where appropriate.
Colour independenceDo not make colour the only way to communicate status or meaning.
MotionUse animation purposefully and consider users who need reduced motion.
Plain languageUse clear structure, headings and understandable content.

Knowledge check

Which approach better supports accessible communication?

Apply

Audit content and media in Figma or Framer

Task

Choose a screen containing imagery, video, icons, status colours or motion. Identify where meaning depends on a single sensory channel.

Deliver

Improve the content with appropriate alternatives, captions/transcript requirements, clearer text and non-colour indicators. Document assumptions and content ownership.

Check the final implementation where possible; a design annotation communicates intent but does not prove the accessibility of the shipped experience.
