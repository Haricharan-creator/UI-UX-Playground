Accessibility Testing & Validation

Learn how to combine automated checks, manual interaction testing and representative user feedback to validate accessibility without treating any single method as proof.

Learn

Validation needs multiple lenses

Automated tools can identify some issues efficiently, while manual keyboard testing, zoom/text-size checks, semantic inspection and assistive-technology testing can reveal other barriers. People with relevant lived experience can uncover problems that tools may miss.

Designer question: What can this test method prove, what can it not prove, and what additional evidence do I need before claiming the experience works?

Practical validation layers

Automated checksUse appropriate tools to catch detectable issues such as some contrast or structural problems.
Keyboard testMove through key flows without a pointer and inspect focus order and visibility.
Zoom/text testIncrease text or zoom and check reflow, clipping and task completion.
Assistive technologyWhere relevant, test semantics and announcements with appropriate technology.
Content reviewCheck labels, instructions, alternatives, error messages and plain language.
User evidenceWhen feasible, include people with relevant access needs in research or validation.

Apply

Run an accessibility validation session

Task

Take a previously designed Figma/Framer flow and create a small validation plan: success criteria, methods, participants or reviewers, environments and evidence to capture.

Deliver

Record findings by method, distinguish confirmed issues from assumptions, assign follow-up actions and state what remains unvalidated.

Do not overclaim from a small test. Report the scope, environment and limitations alongside the findings.
