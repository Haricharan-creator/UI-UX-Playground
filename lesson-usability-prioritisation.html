Prioritising UX Findings

Learn how to move from evidence to an explicit, transparent prioritisation decision without pretending that every issue can be reduced to a single universal score.

Learn

Prioritisation is a decision framework

Not every finding can be fixed at once. Prioritisation should make the criteria visible: user impact, frequency or reach, task criticality, evidence confidence, effort, risk and product context. Different teams may reasonably weigh these factors differently.

Designer question: Which criteria matter for this decision, what evidence supports them, and what trade-off are we making?
See

Useful prioritisation dimensions

User impactHow meaningfully does the issue affect people?
ReachHow often or how broadly does the issue occur?
Task criticalityDoes it affect an important or time-sensitive task?
Evidence confidenceHow strong and direct is the supporting evidence?
EffortWhat implementation/design effort may be required?
Risk & contextAre there business, accessibility, compliance or technical constraints?

Which approach produces a more defensible prioritisation decision?

Apply

Prioritise findings from your Figma/Framer test

Task

Take 3–5 findings from the previous lesson. Define your prioritisation criteria before deciding their order.

Deliver

For each finding record evidence, impact, reach/context, confidence, effort assumptions, proposed action and the trade-off behind the decision.

Do not present a prioritisation as an objective truth. It is a documented decision based on the chosen criteria and available evidence.
