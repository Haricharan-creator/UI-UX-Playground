Accessible Forms

Learn how to design forms that help people understand what information is needed, recover from errors and complete tasks using different input methods and assistive technologies.

Learn

A form is a conversation

Good form design explains what is required, accepts appropriate input, preserves user effort and gives specific recovery guidance. Accessibility depends on structure and behaviour as well as visual styling.

Designer question: Can a person identify each field, understand the expected input, move through the form predictably and recover from an error without losing their work?

Accessible form essentials

LabelsGive every control a clear, persistent label or equivalent accessible name.
InstructionsExplain format and requirements before or where they are needed.
GroupingGroup related controls and questions with meaningful structure.
ErrorsIdentify the problem and provide a useful correction path.
FocusKeep keyboard focus visible and logically ordered.
PreservationAvoid making users re-enter information after an error.

Apply

Build an accessible form in Figma or Framer

Task

Choose a sign-up, checkout or contact flow. Review labels, instructions, grouping, required fields, error states, focus order and recovery.

Deliver

Create the normal, focus, validation-error and success states. Include realistic content and show how user-entered information is preserved.

Validate keyboard behaviour and the implemented semantics when the prototype becomes a real interface; a visual mockup alone cannot prove accessibility.
