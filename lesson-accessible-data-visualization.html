Accessible Data Visualization

Learn how charts, graphs and dashboards can communicate patterns without making colour, shape or visual comparison the only way to understand the data.

Learn

Make the insight available in more than one way

Charts can make complex information easier to understand, but visual encoding can also create barriers. Important values, trends, categories and conclusions should have appropriate textual or tabular support.

Designer question: If someone cannot distinguish the chart colours or cannot perceive the graphic, can they still understand the important information and make the intended decision?
See

Accessible visualization essentials

Direct labelsUse meaningful labels or legends that do not require guesswork.
Text alternativeProvide a concise explanation of the important trend, comparison or conclusion.
Data tableProvide underlying data when the task requires exact values or comparison.
Colour independenceDo not make colour the sole encoding for categories, status or differences.
Patterns & markersUse additional distinguishable encodings where appropriate, especially for overlapping series.
Scale & contextUse honest scales, units and context so the visualization is understandable.

Knowledge check

Which approach gives people more than one route to the meaning of a chart?

Apply

Audit a dashboard in Figma or Framer

Task

Choose a chart or dashboard. Identify every important insight and note how it is encoded: colour, position, size, shape, text or interaction.

Deliver

Add direct labels or a meaningful summary, check colour independence and define a table/data view when the task needs exact values.

Validate the implemented chart with realistic data and test whether users can recover the intended insight without relying on one visual cue.
