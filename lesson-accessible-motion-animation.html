Accessible Motion & Animation

Learn how motion can explain change and hierarchy while also creating distraction, discomfort or barriers when it is unnecessary, unexpected or difficult to control.

Learn

Motion should communicate, not demand attention

Animation can show relationships, state changes and continuity. But motion can also compete with reading, create discomfort or make an interaction harder to control. Good UX treats motion as purposeful and gives users appropriate control.

Designer question: Does this motion help the person understand what changed, and can the experience remain usable when motion is reduced or unavailable?

Motion accessibility essentials

PurposeUse motion to explain state, spatial relationships or feedback rather than decoration alone.
Reduce intensityAvoid unnecessary large, rapid or continuous movement.
User controlWhere appropriate, provide ways to pause, stop or reduce non-essential motion.
Essential meaningDo not make important information depend only on animation.
TransitionsKeep state changes understandable even when transitions are shortened or removed.
ContextConsider motion across onboarding, scrolling, navigation and notifications.

Task

Choose a flow with transitions, loading, scrolling or feedback animation. Classify each motion as explanatory, feedback, decorative or continuous.

Deliver

Create an appropriate reduced-motion version and document which information remains available without animation.

Validate both the animated and reduced-motion experience. Check whether timing, hierarchy and task completion remain understandable.
