Accessible Audio & Media Controls

Learn how video and audio experiences can provide usable controls, captions, transcripts and alternatives so people can understand and operate media in different environments.

Learn

Media needs more than a play button

Accessible media combines understandable controls with alternatives for content that is communicated through sound or visuals. Designers also need to consider autoplay, timing, volume, captions and keyboard or assistive technology access.

Designer question: Can someone understand the important media content and control playback without depending on one sensory channel or a precise input method?
See

Media accessibility essentials

CaptionsProvide synchronized text for spoken dialogue and relevant audio information.
TranscriptsOffer a text alternative when people need to read or search the content.
Audio descriptionWhere needed, describe important visual information that is not conveyed through dialogue.
ControlsMake play, pause, volume, seek and other essential controls understandable and operable.
AutoplayAvoid unexpected playback that can distract, startle or interfere with assistive technology.
TimingConsider whether people can pause, replay or adjust media when content moves at a fixed pace.

Task

Design a video or audio component with default, playing, paused, loading, muted and error states. Map captions, transcript and alternative-content paths.

Deliver

Prototype the controls and document keyboard access, focus states, timing, autoplay behaviour and recovery from loading or playback failure.

Validate the component at different viewport sizes and with realistic content lengths rather than only placeholder media.
