Design Critique

Learn how to give and receive design feedback that is specific, evidence-based and useful for improving the work rather than judging the designer.

Learn

Critique the work, not the person

A strong critique connects an observation to a user need, UX principle, evidence or design goal. It avoids vague preference statements and turns feedback into a question or actionable improvement.

Critique formula: Observation → impact or evidence → principle/goal → question or recommendation.
See

Four lenses for a useful critique

GoalWhat is the user trying to accomplish?
EvidenceWhat do we actually observe or know?
PrincipleWhich UX/UI principle is relevant?
ActionWhat could be explored or changed?
Weak feedback

“This looks confusing and I don't like the layout.”

Mostly personal preference; little actionable evidence.
Useful feedback

“The primary action is visually similar to secondary actions, so users may not recognise the next step quickly. Could we test stronger hierarchy or a clearer grouping?”

Connects observation, impact and a design direction without prescribing one answer.

Apply

Critique your Figma or Framer work

Task

Choose one screen or flow you created. Write three critiques using observation, impact, principle and a question.

Peer review

Ask another learner to critique it. Record what they observed, what you changed, and which feedback you intentionally did not adopt.

The goal is not to accept every comment. The goal is to make design decisions consciously and explain the evidence behind them.
