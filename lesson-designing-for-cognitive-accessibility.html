Cognitive Accessibility & Clear UX

Learn how to reduce unnecessary cognitive load through clear language, predictable structure, progressive disclosure, helpful feedback and forgiving interactions.

Learn

Make the interface easier to understand

People may experience cognitive load differently because of context, stress, fatigue, unfamiliarity, language, environment or individual needs. Good UX reduces unnecessary complexity without assuming everyone thinks or learns the same way.

Designer question: What information, choices or steps can be made clearer so the person can focus on the task instead of figuring out the interface?
See

Cognitive accessibility essentials

Clear languageUse familiar words, concise instructions and meaningful headings.
PredictabilityKeep navigation, labels and interaction patterns consistent.
ChunkingBreak complex information into manageable groups.
Progressive disclosureReveal complexity when it becomes relevant rather than all at once.
ForgivenessSupport undo, confirmation and recovery where errors are costly.
Helpful feedbackExplain system status and next steps in understandable language.

Apply

Reduce cognitive load in Figma or Framer

Task

Choose a complex flow such as onboarding, checkout or settings. Identify unnecessary choices, jargon, memory demands and confusing transitions.

Deliver

Rework the flow using clearer labels, grouping, progressive disclosure, helpful feedback and recovery. Explain what changed and why.

Test with representative users where possible. Avoid assuming that a “simpler” interface is automatically better; simplicity must preserve the task and needed control.
