52. Cognitive Accessibility

Reduce unnecessary mental effort so people can understand, remember and complete tasks with confidence.

LEARN

Make the next action easier to understand

Consistent patterns, plain instructions, visible status and manageable choices reduce cognitive load. Do not make users remember information that the interface can show at the right moment.

Designer question: What must the user remember or infer here that the interface could make visible?

SEE
  • Use familiar patterns and consistent placement.
  • Break complex tasks into meaningful steps.
  • Keep instructions close to the decision they support.
  • Show progress, status and consequences.
  • Preserve entered information during errors.
CHECK

Which choice reduces unnecessary cognitive load?

APPLY — FIGMA / FRAMER

Take a multi-step flow. Highlight every place users must remember, infer or backtrack. Redesign three points to expose the needed information at the moment of action. Capture V1 and V2 screenshots.

Loop: V1 → Cognitive-load review → V2 → Task test → Reflect
