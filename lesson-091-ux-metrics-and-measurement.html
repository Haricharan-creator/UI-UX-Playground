Lesson 091: UX Metrics & Measurement

Learn how to connect UX goals to measurable signals without reducing user experience to a single number.

Learn

Measure the outcome, not just the activity

A metric is useful when it helps answer a product or UX question. Start with the user and business outcome, then choose evidence that can reveal whether the experience is improving. Quantitative signals and qualitative evidence work together.

Designer question: What decision will this measurement help us make, and what evidence would tell us that the experience changed?
See

Measurement essentials

GoalDefine the user or product outcome before selecting a metric.
Task successMeasure whether people can complete an intended task.
EfficiencyConsider time, effort, errors or steps when they matter to the task.
AdoptionTrack meaningful use of a feature rather than activity alone.
Qualitative evidenceUse interviews, observations and usability findings to explain why a signal changed.
GuardrailsPair primary metrics with measures that help detect unintended effects.

Which approach is most useful when defining a UX metric?

Apply

Build a measurement plan in Figma or Framer

Task

Choose one redesigned flow. Write the UX goal, target user behaviour, primary success signal, supporting qualitative evidence and possible guardrail.

Deliver

Create an annotated flow showing where evidence could be collected. State what result would trigger further investigation rather than declaring a design automatically successful.

Keep the distinction clear between a metric, an interpretation and a design decision. Avoid treating correlation as proof of causation.
